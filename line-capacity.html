How it works

How much line fits on a spool depends on the line's diameter, not its breaking strain. Halve the diameter and about four times as much line fits. Reel makers use the same volume rule when they print capacity on the spool:

length × diameter² = spool volume (constant)

The calculator takes that volume from your reel's rating, sets aside room for the main line you want, and fills the rest with backing. Lengths are rounded to whole metres, because spooling is never more precise than that.

FAQ

Where do I find my reel's rated capacity?

It's printed on the spool or in the manual as a diameter and length pair, for example 0.25 mm / 150 m. Enter that pair as your reel's rating. If your reel only lists pound test and yards, convert the line size first.

Why put backing under braid?

Backing is cheap mono that fills the bottom of the spool, so you don't pay for braid you'll never cast. It also stops braid slipping on a bare spool and gives the braid a firm bed to lay on.

Does it work for braid, mono and fluorocarbon?

Yes. The maths uses only diameter, so any line works. For braid, the standard PE diameter is usually closer to reality than the diameter printed on the spool.

How accurate is the result?

Expect it to be within a few metres. Winding tension, spool shape and how evenly the line lays all change real capacity, so spool a little less than the result and check the fill by eye.
