    How the zones work

    A rod's casting weight is the range its blank is built to load. Lures in the middle half of that range bend the rod properly and give the longest, most accurate casts. Near the ends it still works, with some trade-off. Below the minimum the rod barely flexes, and above the maximum a hard cast can break it.

    sweet spot = middle 50% of the range

    This is a rule of thumb. Fast-action rods often feel best in the upper half, softer ones lower down. Weigh your lures if the packet only gives size.

    FAQ

    What does casting weight on a rod mean?

    It's the lure weight range the blank is designed to cast, for example 5-21 g. Below it the rod won't bend enough to load. Above it you risk breaking the blank.

    Can I cast a lure heavier than the maximum?

    A slightly heavy lure on a gentle lob usually survives, but a hard cast can snap the blank, and many makers won't cover that under warranty.

    My rod is rated in ounces. How do I convert?

    1 oz is 28.35 g. A 1/8-1/2 oz rod is about 3.5-14 g.
